## Summary
This changeset refines the visual styling across several frontend tables and dashboard cards by stripping colored status badges in favor of cleaner typography. It also updates route guards and sidebar navigation to restrict access to lending and tenant modules strictly to their designated companies. While the typography cleanup reduces visual noise, the changeset introduces access lockouts for audit and owner roles under the aggregate company scope, adds an overly broad substring match for JK Property, strips essential company and reference identifiers from multi company tables, and leaves behind several unused imports and variables.

## Major
### 🟠 Lockout of Lending and Tenant modules for Audit and Owner, `frontend/src/components/layout/RouteGuards.tsx:179`
**Problem**: The route guards `LendingRouteGuard` and `TenantRouteGuard` removed the role check allowing audit users to inspect modules across companies. They now evaluate only `!isLendingEnabledCompany(targetCompany)` and `!isTenantEnabledCompany(targetCompany)`. In aggregate mode where `targetCompany` is `All Companies` (the default login scope for audit and owner accounts), both functions return false. Furthermore, `navItems.ts:223` removed the fallback that surfaces both modules in the sidebar for audit accounts under aggregate view.
**Why it matters**: When an audit or owner user signs in under `All Companies`, they are blocked from navigating to `/lending` or `/tenants`, being redirected immediately to `/dashboard` without feedback. This violates Spec 0029 (AC-4 and AC-5), which mandates that the Owner role retains complete view visibility across all modules and companies. It also hides these operational domains from auditors unless they switch active company scope manually.
**Suggested fix**: Restore the audit and owner bypass in both route guards so users holding global oversight roles can access `/lending` and `/tenants` when `targetCompany` is `All Companies`. Reintroduce navigation links in `navItems.ts` for audit and owner roles when in aggregate company scope.

### 🟠 Overly broad matching for JK Property company names, `frontend/src/config/companyFeatures.ts:34`
**Problem**: In `isJKPropertyCompany`, the condition includes an unanchored clause `normalized.includes('vertures')`. Because this clause is evaluated as an independent condition without requiring `jk`, any company whose name contains the substring `vertures` will evaluate as true.
**Why it matters**: Any third party company with `vertures` in its name would be falsely identified as JK Property and Ventures. This grants unauthorized access to tenant modules while erroneously stripping access to sales and lending features. Line 35 already covers the intended typo by checking `(normalized.includes('jk') && (normalized.includes('property') || normalized.includes('vertures') || normalized.includes('ventures')))`.
**Suggested fix**: Remove the standalone `normalized.includes('vertures') ||` condition on line 34, relying on the anchored conjunction on line 35.

### 🟠 Elimination of company and reference identifiers in multi company tables, `frontend/src/pages/PettyCashPage.tsx:1526`
**Problem**: The changeset removes company names and reference numbers (`refNo` and `referenceNo`) from the particulars table cells in `PettyCashPage.tsx`, `RecentTransactionsCard.tsx`, and `TransactionPage.tsx`. None of these tables have dedicated columns for Company or Reference Number.
**Why it matters**: In centralized multi company views (`All Companies`), users reviewing financial transactions or petty cash entries cannot determine which operating entity owns an expense or deposit. For example, generic particulars like Office Supplies or Sales appear completely anonymous in the table list. Auditors and finance officers cannot match line items against paper vouchers without opening the detail drawer for every record individually.
**Suggested fix**: Conditionally render the company name and reference number under particulars whenever the active company is `All Companies` or when transactions span multiple entities.

## Minor
### 🟡 Bank account dropdown lost active indicator styling, `frontend/src/pages/TransactionPage.tsx:437`
**Problem**: In `BankAccountFilterDropdown`, the dynamic classes `selectedAcc ? 'border-primary/50 bg-primary/5' : 'border-stroke/60'` were replaced with static `border-stroke/60`.
**Why it matters**: When a user selects a bank account filter, the button provides no visual indication that a filter is currently active. This creates an inconsistent user experience with the neighboring `TransactionDateRangeControl`, which continues to highlight its border and background when active.
**Suggested fix**: Restore the conditional active styling on the filter trigger button when `selectedAcc` is truthy.

### 🟡 Unused subtitle variable in ledger row renders, `frontend/src/pages/TransactionPage.tsx:3249`
**Problem**: In three separate locations (`TransactionPage.tsx:3249`, `3568`, and `3858`), `subtitle` is destructured from `getBankLedgerParticulars(tx)`, but the JSX markup rendering `{subtitle}` was removed.
**Why it matters**: Destructuring variables that are never read increases cognitive load and can trigger linter warnings.
**Suggested fix**: Either remove `subtitle` from the destructuring assignment or restore its rendering if metadata context is retained.

### 🟡 Unused CATEGORY_COLORS import and categoryColor variable, `frontend/src/components/dashboard/AuditTransactionModal.tsx:37`
**Problem**: `AuditTransactionModal.tsx` imports `CATEGORY_COLORS` on line 37 and computes `const categoryColor` on line 430, but neither is referenced in the component following the replacement of the category pill badge with plain text.
**Why it matters**: Dead imports and unused variables clutter the codebase and can cause compiler or linter diagnostics.
**Suggested fix**: Remove the unused `CATEGORY_COLORS` import on line 37 and delete the `categoryColor` assignment on line 430.

### 🟡 Unused Landmark import after removing bank breakdown, `frontend/src/pages/SalesPage.tsx:27`
**Problem**: Line 27 in `SalesPage.tsx` imports `Landmark` from `lucide-react`, but the icon was only used in the deleted bank breakdown component.
**Why it matters**: Leaving dead icon imports increases bundle overhead and clutters component dependencies.
**Suggested fix**: Remove `Landmark` from the `lucide-react` import statement on line 27.

## Strengths
- Clean visual simplification: Transitioning from heavy colored pill badges to restrained semantic text colors (such as emerald-700, rose-700, and amber-700) significantly declutters dense financial tables and establishes a cohesive aesthetic across ledger views.
- Streamlined expanded row layout: Eliminating the redundant bank breakdown cards inside `SalesPage.tsx` reduces visual noise and allows users to focus directly on itemized transaction records.
