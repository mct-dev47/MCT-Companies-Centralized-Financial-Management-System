## Blockers
### 🔴 Cross company loan and petty cash data leak when `company_id` is omitted, `backend/app/api/v1/endpoints/transactions.py:576`
**Problem**: When a restricted user (`allowed_cids` is not `None`, such as a supervisor assigned to one company) requests `GET /api/v1/transactions` without passing `company_id` or `company_name` (`target_cid` is `None`), Section 5 (`transactions.py:541`) fetches every loan where either `Loan.borrower` or `Loan.lender` is in `allowed_cids`. Lines `576` (`if not target_cid or ln.borrower == target_cid:`), `605` (`if ln.lender and (not target_cid or ln.lender == target_cid) and ln.lender != ln.borrower:`), and `645` (`if ln.lender and (not target_cid or ln.lender == target_cid):`) emit both sides of the loan and all lender repayments without verifying that the counterparty company is in `allowed_cids`. Similarly, in Section 3 (`transactions.py:299, 329`), `BankAccount.company_id.in_(allowed_cids)` exposes another company's `PettyCashTopUp` row to a user who owns only the funding bank.
**Why it matters**: Any restricted company user calling `GET /api/v1/transactions` without a company filter receives unauthorized counterparty company names, bank account IDs, loan descriptions, finance remarks, and audit remarks across company boundaries.
**Suggested fix**: Enforce `allowed_cids is None or company_id in allowed_cids` on every emitted borrower, lender, and repayment row in Section 5 (`576, 605, 645`) and enforce company isolation for cross company petty cash funding rows in Section 3 (`299, 329`).

### 🔴 Approved petty cash topups marked Pending with zero withdrawal and broken transfer states, `backend/app/api/v1/endpoints/transactions.py:356`
**Problem**: Section 3 (`transactions.py:344-367`) breaks petty cash ledger accuracy in four ways:
1. Line `356` (`elif "pending" in st_lower or st_lower == "approved":`) places `"Approved"` rows into `status="Pending"`, `withdrawal=0.0`, `awaitingAudit=True`, `auditStatus="Pending Audit"`, and `paidAmount=0.0`. Because reimbursements already exit at line `323` unless `status == "reimbursed"`, only `topup` records reach line `356` with `"approved"`, and `PettyCashService.audit_review` (`backend/app/services/petty_cash_service.py:1873-1881`) marks topups `"Approved"` as their final state and immediately withdraws cash from the bank via `_disburse_to_bank`.
2. `"Return Pending"` still matches `"pending" in st_lower` at line `356`, zeroing out a bank withdrawal that already happened (`petty_cash_service.py:2198, 2290-2310`).
3. `"Transfer In Transit"` (`344-349`) sets `awaitingAudit=True` and `auditStatus="Pending Audit"`, causing `TransactionPage.tsx:862` (`!t.awaitingAudit`) to exclude the real bank withdrawal from the Bank Ledger, while `"Cancelled"` falls into `else:` (`362-367`) as `"Paid"`.
4. For cross company petty cash funding (`297, 329`), `transactions.py:102` skips loading `all_comps` when `type=petty_cash` (producing `"Unknown Company"`), and Section 3 emits a single withdrawal row under `p.company_id` (the recipient) with the funder's `bankAccountId`, which `TransactionPage.tsx:890, 1367` filters out for the funding company. Meanwhile, `PettyCashService.reimburse` (`petty_cash_service.py:2113-2124`) creates an `Expense` (`payment_method="Bank Reimbursement"`, `linked_topup_id`) that still appears in Section 2 alongside the `petty_` row in Section 3.
**Why it matters**: Every approved petty cash topup is omitted from Bank Ledger balances and displayed as `Pending`, intercompany transfers show inverted or missing bank balances, and bank funded reimbursements appear twice in `All Transactions`.
**Suggested fix**: Treat `"approved"` topups as `Paid` and `Verified` with full bank withdrawal, keep `"Transfer In Transit"` and `"Return Pending"` withdrawals active in the Bank Ledger (`awaitingAudit=False`, `auditStatus="Verified"`), exclude `"Cancelled"`, split cross company petty cash fundings into funder withdrawal and recipient deposit rows (with `all_comps` loaded whenever petty cash is queried), and exclude `Bank Reimbursement` expenses linked to a `petty_` payout from duplicating Section 3 rows.

### 🟠 Non atomic `BillsService` deletion and swallowed Supabase errors, `backend/app/services/bills_service.py:652`
**Problem**: Inside `BillsService._remove_funding_expense` (`bills_service.py:636-670`), `reconcile_expense_bank` and `BankService.purge_linked_entries` invoke `self.db.commit()` (`backend/app/services/bank_service.py:777, 880`) inside the `for payment in list(bill.payments or []):` loop (`bills_service.py:437-438`) before `delete_bill` (`439-440`) or `delete_payment` (`679-680`) commits, while Supabase delete failures (`657-663`) and bank purge failures (`665-668`) are caught and only logged as warnings.
**Why it matters**: A failure midway through deleting a multi payment bill permanently commits partial expense and bank reversals while leaving the bill and remaining payments intact, and Supabase failures leave ghost remote expenses.
**Suggested fix**: Support `commit=False` on `BankService` reconciliation and purge helpers so all SQL mutations commit once at the end of `delete_bill` or `delete_payment`, and handle remote delete failures consistently before committing.

### 🟠 Unbounded full table queries across all six sections, `backend/app/api/v1/endpoints/transactions.py:79`
**Problem**: `list_transactions` leaves `limit` defaulting to `None` (`transactions.py:79, 753-754`) and `frontend/src/services/transactionService.ts:24-50` passes no `limit`, so every call executes `.all()` across all six source tables (`134, 225, 306, 451, 548, 693`) before sorting and slicing in Python. In addition, `offset > 0` without `limit` is ignored (`753`), and Section 4 (`482-493`) loads all unlinked `BankTransaction` rows and filters string dates in Python.
**Why it matters**: As table row counts grow, default ledger loads incur full table memory allocation, latency spikes, and wasted DB I/O.
**Suggested fix**: Set a bounded default `limit`, apply `offset` even when `limit` uses the default bound, and push date bounds down to SQL queries wherever possible.
